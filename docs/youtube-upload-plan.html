YouTube upload plan

A plan for publishing the pattern videos on the channel, starting with Circuit Breaker. It follows the rules in youtube-upload-spec.md. This is a plan only: nothing is uploaded until the channel owner does it.

The plan, step by step

  1. Pick the files from the project's local build (none of them are in git):
  2. the video: the -amy-slow.mp4 (older projects) or the plain .mp4 (newer projects). The .mp4 contains picture and narration together, so no separate audio is uploaded; the .m4a beside it is an audio-only copy and stays local;
  3. the thumbnail: that project's own docs/thumbnail.png (every project has one);
  4. optionally, the captions: the .srt with the same name as the video (text, not audio).
  5. Prepare the text from the project's docs/youtube.md:
  6. the title as written;
  7. the description, with every http:// and https:// removed, and the chapters timed from the uploaded video's own .srt;
  8. the tags as written.
  9. Upload in YouTube Studio: Create, then Upload videos, then select the .mp4.
  10. Details page:
  11. paste the title and description;
  12. Thumbnail: Upload file, then choose the project's docs/thumbnail.png;
  13. Playlists: the playlist for the project's category (see the table below); if it does not exist yet, create it there with New playlist, Public;
  14. Audience: select "No, it's not made for kids";
  15. Paid promotion (under Show more): select "No, my video doesn't include paid promotion";
  16. Altered content (under Show more): select No. The narration is a synthetic text-to-speech voice, but it imitates no real person, and the video is slides, not realistic footage, so it does not meet YouTube's definition of altered or synthetic content;
  17. under Show more: tags, language English, category Science & Technology.
  18. Video elements: optionally add the captions (Upload file, With timing, the .srt), then an end screen and a playlist card.
  19. Check before saving: the project's thumbnail is showing, "not made for kids" and altered content "No" are selected, the description has no http:// or https://, the chapters start at 00:00, and the captions are attached if used.
  20. Visibility: choose Public, tick Set as instant Premiere, and save. Premiere cannot be added once the video is public.
  21. Check after publishing: the chapters appear under the progress bar, the captions are in sync if added, and the first and last minute sound clean. Share the link only after 1080p processing has finished, because code slides are unreadable at lower quality.
  22. Repeat per project, a few at a time, in learning order for the playlist.

Title

Circuit Breaker in Java - When a Service Stops

Description (ready to paste)

No http:// or https://, and the chapters are timed for the amy-slow video.

Circuit breaker pattern in Java 21: a circuit breaker watches calls to another service, and after too many failures stops calling it for a while and fails fast instead, like a fuse that trips. We start from a service that has stopped answering — not refusing, which would be easy, but simply not replying. The retry loop is presented fairly: no bug, the right page, every test passing, and nine seconds spent producing a page you could have had immediately. The third cost is the one that actually breaks the shop: while those threads wait, checkout cannot get one. We use a fuse box as the analogy and then build the breaker as three questions inside one method, with the states named as a wire rather than a door — closed, open, half open — and a success that resets rather than decrements. Six pages, three calls, three refusals, and the clock stops moving. Then the hinge: a breaker makes failures fast, not invisible. Checkout has no fallback, so speed buys it a fast honest no, and the same wiring with one different catch block prints a receipt for money that never moved.
